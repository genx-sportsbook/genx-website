NBA Playoff Rollercoaster: Who Really Saw the Celtics Coming?

The 2026 NBA playoffs delivered drama, upsets, and — for savvy market participants — a masterclass in value extraction. We’re dissecting the wildest shifts in championship odds, from the early favorites' collapses to the improbable surges that redefined the season.

38%Nuggets' Peak Implied Probability

Remember when the Denver Nuggets were trading at an implied probability of 38% to win the NBA championship back in early April? That feels like a lifetime ago, doesn't it? The postseason, as it always does, shredded preseason narratives and mid-season certainties, offering a brutal lesson in market volatility and the beautiful chaos of professional basketball. For those tracking the prediction markets, the journey from April 15th to the final buzzer of the NBA Finals was less a steady climb and more a series of gut-wrenching lurches, rewarding patience and punishing hubris.

The Celtics' eventual triumph, while not a complete shock by the time they reached the Conference Finals, was a significant departure from their pre-playoff pricing. Heading into the first round, Boston was hovering around an 18% implied probability to lift the Larry O'Brien trophy. This put them behind the aforementioned Nuggets, the Milwaukee Bucks (estimated 25%), and even the resurgent Dallas Mavericks (estimated 20%). The market, it seemed, was still skeptical of their regular-season dominance translating into playoff grit, a ghost of seasons past haunting their valuation. Yet, they methodically dismantled every opponent, exposing the market's collective blind spot with each passing series.

The Favorites' Folly: Denver's Dive and Milwaukee's Meltdown

The biggest story in terms of market value destruction has to be the Denver Nuggets. After a dominant regular season and a strong start to the playoffs, their price peaked. We saw them hit that 38% implied probability after securing the top seed in the West. Then came the unexpected, brutal sweep at the hands of the Phoenix Suns in the Western Conference Semifinals. In the span of just seven days, their championship odds plummeted from 38% to effectively 0%. That's a staggering $380 return on a $1000 bet evaporating faster than a bad parlay. Anyone holding significant 'Nuggets to Win' positions from earlier in the season took a bath of epic proportions, illustrating the inherent risks of riding a perceived favorite too hard, too early.

Milwaukee followed a similar, if slightly less dramatic, trajectory. Their 25% implied probability heading into the playoffs was a testament to Giannis Antetokounmpo's MVP-caliber season and a relatively smooth path in the East. However, a tough matchup against the Miami Heat, combined with some key injuries, saw their odds erode quickly. By the time they were bounced in six games, their market share was long gone. The lesson here is clear: even seemingly invincible teams are vulnerable, and the market often overprices the established giants, leaving juicy value elsewhere.

The Rise of the Underdogs (and the Value They Offered)

While Denver and Milwaukee were burning money, other teams offered remarkable upside. The Boston Celtics, as mentioned, were the ultimate payoff. But let's not forget the Phoenix Suns. Their pre-playoff implied probability was a paltry 7%. By the time they were battling it out in the Western Conference Finals, their odds had soared to an estimated 30%. Smart money, or perhaps just prescient fans, who backed the Suns early saw their positions multiply. This wasn't just about picking the winner; it was about identifying undervalued assets capable of making a deep run.

Even the teams that didn't ultimately win provided significant trading opportunities. The Dallas Mavericks, for example, started at around 20% but saw their odds briefly touch 35% after an impressive first-round upset. Understanding these shifts, and recognizing when a team is being over- or undervalued in real-time, is the essence of market intelligence. The 2026 NBA Playoffs, with its wild swings and unpredictable outcomes, was a masterclass in this very dynamic.

Ultimately, the 2026 NBA playoffs reinforced a fundamental truth about prediction markets: they are living, breathing entities, constantly adjusting to new information, injuries, and sheer on-court performance. The Celtics' journey from a respected contender to an undisputed champion, and the dramatic falls of early favorites, prove that no outcome is truly predetermined. For those willing to dig past the headlines and understand the underlying value, the NBA postseason continues to be one of the most fertile grounds for market analysis and, potentially, significant returns.
