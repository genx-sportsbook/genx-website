Of course, no model is perfect, and the beauty of prediction markets lies in their dynamic nature. For instance, tracking the 'implied volatility' of team odds can be a powerful indicator. A high implied volatility suggests the market is still undecided, or reacting sharply to new information. Low implied volatility, especially for top contenders, indicates a strong, data-backed consensus that is harder to shake. The current market for South Africa, despite its upward trend, still shows a higher implied volatility than, say, Australia, suggesting there's still room for significant price movement. The objective isn't to beat the algorithm, but to understand its output and identify its temporary blind spots.
