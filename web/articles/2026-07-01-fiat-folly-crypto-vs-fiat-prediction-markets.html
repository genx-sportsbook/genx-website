Liquidity, Transparency, and the Tyranny of the Vig

One of the biggest differences lies in how liquidity is generated and priced. Centralised sportsbooks are market makers. They set the odds, and they adjust them based on their risk exposure and desired profit margin. This means their odds aren't a true reflection of market sentiment; they're a reflection of the book's exposure. If too many people are betting on one side, they'll move the line to balance their books, often offering less favourable odds than the 'true' probability would dictate. This 'tyranny of the vig' means you're almost always getting a worse price than you should be.

On a crypto prediction market like Genx-Sportsbook, liquidity is user-driven. Traders themselves act as market makers, providing liquidity by offering odds at which they are willing to buy or sell. This creates a more efficient market where odds converge closer to true probabilities, driven by collective intelligence rather than a centralised entity's risk management. The fees are also dramatically lower. While fiat books charge a hidden vig, Genx-Sportsbook operates on a transparent, low-percentage fee structure (e.g., 1-2% on winning trades, often less for high-volume traders) that is openly stated and applied uniformly.
